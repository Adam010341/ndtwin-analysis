**OVS declared (5 values).** `POST /ndt/link_failure_detected` on edge `1:1↔5:1`
(one of OVS4's 32 switch-switch edges), 5 trials of a 61 s cycle each. Each trial's
"seconds until DOWN was no longer observed" is the timestamp of the flip back to
`(True, True)` in the probe's own polling, read verbatim from
`r2-20-linkfail-probe.log`: trial 1 25.354 s, trial 2 24.432 s, trial 3 23.414 s,
trial 4 22.455 s, trial 5 21.479 s. Sorted ascending on the chart. The ~0.96 s step
between trials is not noise: the probe's 61 s trial period against the poll's 30 s
period (`61 mod 30 = 1`) means each trial samples a different phase of the same
30 s cycle — see §2.

**P4 declared (4 of 5 values).** Same declaration endpoint, edge `s1:1↔s5:1` (of
P4-4's 32 switch-switch edges), 5 trials × a 20 s observation window each (shorter
than OVS's — see caveat below), from `p4-4-14-linkfail.log`: trial 1 13.558 s,
trial 2 **did not revert inside its 20 s window** (excluded, see caveat), trial 3
1.576 s, trial 4 10.598 s, trial 5 19.624 s. Sorted ascending on the chart:
1.6 / 10.6 / 13.6 / 19.6 s.

**Real cut (1 value).** `sudo -n tc qdisc add dev s1-eth1/s5-eth1 root netem loss
100%` on OVS4 (both link ends), held for a fixed 60 s window, then
`tc qdisc del`. This does not touch the kernel or the API at all — it severs the
link under Mininet directly, independent of anything `link_failure_detected`
declares. Plotted at 60 s (the test window length) with an open arrow-cap and
"≥ 60 s" label, because the honest reading is **right-censored**, not a measured
revert time: the link was still down when the window ended and was brought back
only by removing the `netem` qdisc, not by any self-revert. See §3 for the exact
detection timestamp used.

## 2. The mechanism (why declared failures come back on their own)

Ryu's `GET /v1.0/topology/links` polls every 30 s (observed in `ryu.log` landing at
`:27` and `:57` past the minute); each declared-failure self-revert lands ~0.6 s
after a `:57` poll. `kernel.log` has the `handleLinkFailure] link failed` line for
every declaration but **no matching "recovered" line** for any of the 9 self-reverts
— the only "recovered" lines in the whole run are the probe's own explicit
recovery POSTs. So a declared failure's visible lifetime is a draw from
**0–30 s, uniform, bounded by the poll period** — not a fixed number, and not
something the endpoint or the docs say anything about (`doc/2026-01-02_ndt_api.md`
§1 only says the endpoint "marks the edge DOWN"). Full derivation:
rounds/03-R2-concurrency.md §3-2 (R2-B).

An earlier, rougher pass at the same phenomenon (round 01, OVS128 arm, same
binary, trunk `f943de8f`) saw self-reverts at 0.457 / 9.454 / 18.453 s in 3 of 5
trials — a different phase of the same 30 s cycle, not a contradiction. That
earlier number is **not** plotted here; R2-B's 5/5 with the mechanism nailed down
is the one on the chart.

## 3. The "12.5 s" vs "13.34 s" reconciliation (disclosed, not silently picked)

`rounds/03-R2-concurrency.md` contains **two different numbers** for when the real
cut was first seen down: the summary table in §2 says "T0+12.5 s", but the
detailed walkthrough in §3-2 quotes the raw block as "DOWN t-T0=+13.34s". The raw
log (`r2-21-netem-control.log`) settles it: `T0=1788514617.550689969`, the last
`True` sample is at `T0+12.318 s`, and the first `False,False` sample (1 Hz
polling) is at `1788514630.891669370` = **T0+13.341 s**. There is no sample near
12.5 s anywhere in the log. Per CLAUDE.md's raw-over-narrative rule, this figure
uses the value the raw log actually supports (~13.3 s, bounded between the last-up
and first-down 1 Hz samples) — the "12.5 s" in §2's summary table is not used here
and should be treated as an imprecise paraphrase, not a second measurement. This
does not change the figure's shape: the real cut was still down at both readings,
`tc del` was not issued until T0+59.5 s, and it was back within 1.03 s of removal.

## 4. Caveats

- **The OVS group and the P4 group are two separate lab arms, not repeated
  measurements of one experiment.** OVS declared runs on OVS4 (rounds/03, R2,
  trunk `4088b237`, edge `1:1↔5:1` of its own 32 switch-switch edges); P4
  declared runs on P4-4 (rounds/02, trunk `f943de8f`, edge `s1:1↔s5:1` of a
  *different* 32-edge topology, different lab instance, different time window).
  They share the same kernel binary and the same 30 s Ryu poll mechanism (§2),
  which is why both are plotted against the same 30 s reference line, but do not
  read "P4 vs. OVS" as a paired before/after or an n=9 sample of one thing —
  it is n=5 of one arm and n=4 (of 5) of another, side by side because they
  demonstrate the same mechanism, not because they are the same trial.
- **P4 trial 2 is right-censored, not zero.** It did not flip back within its 20 s
  window. Given the mechanism in §2 (0–30 s uniform), a revert between 20 s and
  30 s is plausible but **not observed** — it is omitted from the chart rather than
  guessed at. This is the reason the P4 group has 4 bars where OVS has 5.
- **The two declared groups' windows are not the same length** (P4: 20 s per
  trial; OVS: ~60 s per trial/cycle). This does not affect the four confirmed P4
  values themselves (each was observed to revert well inside its window), but it
  means "P4 reverts faster than OVS" is **not** a claim this figure makes — the
  P4 group is small (n=4) and its own spread (1.6–19.6 s) already covers most of
  the 0–30 s range the mechanism predicts. Two different edges, two different fabrics.
- **The real-cut bar's length (60 s) is the test window, not a measured
  "time-to-revert."** A real cut may never self-revert; 60 s is simply how long
  this trial watched it stay down before manually restoring it.
